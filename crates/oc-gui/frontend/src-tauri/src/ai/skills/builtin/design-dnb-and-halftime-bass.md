Build a one-bar cell with a clear bass stop and room around the snare.
In drum and bass, test the cell with the fast drum pattern. In halftime
or riddim, leave the space around the backbeat exposed before adding fills.

## Separate pitch weight from motion
Keep a centered, single-voice sub. Give the moving mid layer its own patch
or track, especially if stereo unison or distortion changes its low end.
For a Reese, try a modest saw detune on the mid layer and inspect several
notes for uneven beating. For jump-up or a growl, start with one supported
warp, filter or modulation route and add movement slowly.

Duplicate the preset before experimenting. Query devices_catalog for
the actual oscillator, warp, filter and route options. Try wavetable
position variation on a repeated cell while keeping the amp rhythm
fixed. If a comb or resonant effect changes the heard pitch, reduce its
depth or use it on the mid layer alone. Keep a small amp release for
clean stops and listen before shortening it further.

## Check the junction
Compare sub alone, mid alone and both together under kick and snare.
Listen at the quietest and loudest notes and compare the mono fold-down.
Use audio_analyze for low-band energy and headroom, then check the attack
and spaces in audition. If a bright patch gets brittle on high notes,
compare one milder drive setting at matched loudness before changing
both filter and distortion.
