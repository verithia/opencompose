Keep the DI or untouched recording so you can change the tone later. Audition
a loud palm mute and an open chord before choosing the chain. If you need an
amp processor, inspect the loaded plugin and its input calibration. Set the
input so the hardest playing drives it as intended without digital overload.

## Build width from performances

- Make two separately played takes for a wide rhythm pair when possible.
  Match obvious attacks and releases against the drums. Keep enough natural
  difference to hear a pair and enough agreement to hear one groove.
- Balance the parts before panning. Bring up one side at a time to check
  buzz, hiss and chord definition. Listen to both in mono for smeared
  attacks or a hollow tone. Duplicating one take changes level and placement
  but keeps the same timing and tone.
- Match output loudness after changing drive, gain or cabinet tone. Keep
  the setting where chord movement and pick definition survive.

## Make room in the full band

Start with the bass and drums present. Treat a narrow fizz or resonance
separately from overall bite. If palm mutes bloom only on a few notes, compare
a targeted dynamic band against a static cut. Trim rumble only as far as the
guitar body survives, and move a harsh upper band in small steps.
Use devices_catalog or plugins_params for real parameter names and units.

Compare the same riff at similar loudness with and without each edit.
Check what happens when the vocal enters, when the kick lands on a palm mute,
and when a held chord rings into the next bar.
