Start with bass and drums, then add the lead and piano. Give the ride or brush part a clear pulse.
Where the bass carries roots, use the third and seventh on piano. In C, try F3/C4 for Dm7,
F3/B3 for G7 and E3/B3 for Cmaj7. Add higher colors only where the lead has room.

Use project or pattern swing for eighth-note offbeats if the reference calls for it. Audition with the
bass before adding explicit note offsets. Give the principal drum pulses stable anchors, vary selected
pickups, and keep the stereo image compact if the ride or piano distracts from the lead.

## Four-bar comping study

1. At 112 BPM in 4/4, write Dm7, G7, Cmaj7 and Cmaj7. On `bass/acoustic`, try quarter notes
   D2–F2–A2–F#2, G2–B2–D3–B2, then C3–G2–E2–G2. The F#2 and B2 lead into the next roots.
   Leave space after C3 in bar 4 for the lead or a drum pickup. Use `ride` if the kit maps it.
2. Make a control with the piano shells on every downbeat. In the sparse version, write a short
   response after the lead's first phrase and leave a whole bar without comping. Keep bass, lead
   and drums fixed across both passes. With project swing on, check the pocket before adding
   a second manual delay to those eighth offbeats.
3. Read indices with `tracks_list`, inspect each phrase with `project_extract`, edit with
   `edit_subtree` and run `project_validate`. Use `audition_range` from bar 1 to the exclusive
   end bar 5 for bass and drums, piano and lead, then the whole combo.
4. Hear whether the bass approach resolves, the lead ends before the piano answer, and bar 4 keeps
   its pulse. Restore a timekeeper accent if the pulse fades. Use `tracks_mix` for small piano
   or ride level changes after hearing the full combo. Compare sparse and dense sections before
   adding bus processing.
