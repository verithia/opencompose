Keep the source recordings. Choose a whole take for the lead's emotional arc,
then replace only phrases where another take improves delivery, pitch, or clarity.
Compare candidates against the backing at comparable levels. Check the pickup into
each phrase and the end of the previous phrase before choosing a replacement.

## Work with recorded takes

- Ask the operator to arm the audio track and set the loop or punch range if
  recording is needed. With loop recording, each pass is saved when transport
  stops. Expand Takes and audition individual rows against the arrangement.
- Take solo is session-only. Select the intended span on the take row to make a
  document edit, then inspect the comp row. Earlier unused passes may stay muted.
- For a proposed text edit, inspect the whole project for the top-level audio
  clips. Preserve each source file, take number and group. Confirm each chosen
  segment is audible and competing passes stay muted over the same range.

## Make the seams disappear

Join in a quiet gap or steady sound away from a consonant or pitch change when
possible. If the edit clicks, try a short overlap and equal-power crossfade.
The comp gesture does not add fades. Set crossfade lengths to match the overlap
when using the clip's crossfade field, which acts on both ends of a clip.
Check the other end too. If matching clip copies sum audibly, change the seam.

Audition a continuous sentence across the join, then the adjacent musical phrase.
Listen for repeated consonants, missing inhalations, room-tone jumps and a
sudden change in projection. Level the edited phrase before tuning or compression.
Validate the proposed arrangement and keep the unused takes recoverable.
