Read the guitar and bass attacks before writing the kit. Identify the riff's
main accents, repeated subdivision and phrase ending. Pick the intended
substyle and reference feel rather than use speed as the only genre clue.

## Assign rhythmic jobs
Start with a timekeeper, a backbeat or chosen snare pattern and selected kicks.
Let kick accents reinforce or answer the riff; copying every guitar note is
one option, not a requirement. Compare the riff with and without that doubling.
Keep the heard pulse clear through syncopation and rests.
Choose a slower cymbal pulse when a dense kick figure needs space above it.
Add only the double-kick passages that change the phrase's intended energy.

## Use the kit's articulations
Read assemble-a-sampled-drum-kit and inspect the actual pad and layer schema.
Distinguish snare center, rim, side-stick, open/closed hat and ride bell when
those sources are present. A shorter gate does not create another articulation.
Verify rapid repeated hits across the available velocity layers and alternates.
Do not randomize every hit before the phrase's accents and quieter notes exist.
For an acoustic-player part, check simultaneous hands, feet and impossible
reaches. A deliberate electronic extension can exceed those limits explicitly.

## Compose a fill that arrives
Derive a short fill from the riff's accent pattern or subdivision.
Move it between snare and toms without losing the landing beat.
Leave room to return from a tom run to the next cymbal or snare.
Test a shorter fill against a busier one and keep the one that reinforces form.
Use a choke or a written stop only when the loaded kit can produce it;
otherwise choose an available muted source and state the limitation.

## Verify the band
Validate, then audition drums alone, with guitar and bass, and in the section.
Include the lead-in and first full bar after the fill.
Listen for flams, machine-like repeated timbre, kick tails that hide the bass,
and cymbal wash that removes the riff's definition.
Change articulation, density or note length before compressing every track.
Compare verse and climax so the kit has somewhere to grow.
