Choose the take that tells the story across a whole line. Keep a dry copy and
check the quietest phrase with its accompaniment. Write down which breaths,
slides or rough edges are part of the performance before cleaning anything.

## Build the arrangement around the vocal

- Start with voice and one harmonic instrument. Reduce the backing's
  attacks during fast words. Let a short reply, sustained note or guitar
  figure answer the ends of lines where the lyric leaves room.
- For a busier section, try one hummed or vowel layer on the important words
  before doubling the whole lead. Keep quieter sections sparse so the
  new part feels like a change of perspective.
- Evaluate a harmony in the whole chord and with the lead. Keep a tension
  that resolves in context. Rework one that obscures the melody's landing.

## Keep it close

Balance the lead and accompaniment before adding a long tail. If the guitar
masks a word, check its level or a narrow resonant band during that word.
Make a small change and compare it with simply changing the arrangement.
For uneven phrases, try clip gain before heavy compression. Leave useful
breath and room tone between phrases. Add space on a separate return so the
dry performance can be compared and adjusted independently.

Audition the quiet verse, the chorus entrance and a phrase ending at
similar monitoring levels. Check that the words stay clear and the section
contrast remains audible when the extra harmony or reverb is muted.
