Decide the job of each backing part. A double reinforces the melody, a harmony
changes the chord colour, and an ad-lib answers the lyric. Try one entry at a
key word, cadence or chorus pickup before filling an entire section.

## Arrange the voices

- Keep the lead as a centre reference. Compose harmony against the sounding
  chords and the lead range. Keep a common note when the chord permits it,
  then try contrary or oblique motion to avoid a whole stack moving in parallel.
- Sing or sequence a short harmony with a clear vowel and a comfortable range.
  Place ad-libs in gaps. If lead words become hard to hear, shorten the backing
  sustain or thin its register before lifting the lead again.
- Use independent takes for left and right doubles when available. Set their
  balance at the vocal bus input first. Pan each pair only as wide as the
  arrangement can support. Level and listen in mono before adding depth effects.

## Sketch a derived harmony

An @vocal_harmony block can derive one voice per source clip. Use a monophonic
lead and confirm the active key. The interval counts diatonic steps in an active
scale, with a warning and semitone fallback when no scale is active.
The voice follows the scale and detected melody. Check it against actual chords,
especially altered harmony and passing tones. Use a separate recorded or
programmed part where the sketch chooses an awkward note.
Use the block's level and pan to blend the derived voice on the lead track.

Audition the lead and backs together over the section before and after the
stack enters. Check consonant clarity, the loudest vowel and the final cutoff
in mono and stereo when available.
