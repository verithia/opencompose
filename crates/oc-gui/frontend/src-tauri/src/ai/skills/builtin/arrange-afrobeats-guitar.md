## Build the interlock

- Choose a reference and identify the guitar hook, kick, and the repeating subdivision accents.
  Start with a two- or four-bar guitar figure that leaves an answer space.
- Use a soft pluck for the basic melody and reserve strong strokes for accents that need the harder
  timbre. Confirm that the preset actually responds to velocity.
- Try a quieter muted guitar or pluck on an independent track. Give it an answer rather than a copy
  of the lead, and shorten notes that overlap the lead's attack.
- Establish the kick and backbeat accents, then write the shaker as accent groups. Keep the shaker
  pulse distinct from the kick syncopation. Add a percussion answer only in a deliberate gap.

## Place bass and texture

- Write bass entries around the kick and guitar accents. If they mask one another, shorten the
  overlapping bass note or shift an optional entry before trying dynamic processing.
- Keep the hook central in the arrangement. Use a short pad, another chord register or a reversed
  audio fragment as occasional texture where the phrase leaves space.
- Use pattern variations for the last bar or transition. Change one layer at a time so the core
  pulse remains easy to follow.

## Verify the groove

- Audition guitar and percussion with bass, then the same bars with the vocal or a guide melody.
  Check whether the guitar response fits before the next line.
- If a velocity accent feels too abrupt, compare a smaller gain or velocity contrast on that hit.
  If the rhythm feels busy, remove fills and shorten tails.
- Check a sparse entry and the full section. Keep the shaker and muted guitar audible without
  pushing them forward of the hook.
