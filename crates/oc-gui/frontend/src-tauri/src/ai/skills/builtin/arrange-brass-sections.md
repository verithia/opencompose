## Give each layer a job

- Sketch the tune, answering figure and harmonic foundation. Place the tune where its chosen
  instrument sounds clear at the intended dynamic.
- Try trumpet or another bright source for definition, horn for connected middle lines, and
  trombone or tuba for support. Audition the actual patch across that register. A playable extreme
  may still sound strained or thin.
- Keep low harmony open. Start with a bass foundation and put thirds or extensions higher. Audition
  a deliberate octave double before keeping it.
- For a pop horn stab, agree on the accent and cutoff first. Give a lead, middle voice and low
  voice separate tracks when their balance or release needs to differ.

## Program attacks and swells

- Use a short patch for tongued accents and a sustain for held notes. Compare the timing of the
  rendered attack against the rhythm section before nudging individual parts.
- Verify any documented patch controller with a held note and a rendered ramp. Dynamics may
  crossfade timbre while expression changes level. If control events are unsupported, choose a
  recorded swell or shape track gain while keeping expectations about timbre modest.
- Keep trumpet peaks and low-brass accents for important moments. Write rests or stagger entries in
  long passages so the section has space.

## Check the ensemble

- Balance the parts dry. Use a common room only if it helps place them together, especially when
  samples already contain recorded space.
- Audition the loudest stab, the quietest entry and the release into a rest at the same playback
  level as the baseline.
- If low strings and brass compete, test one layer at a time and revise the register, attack or
  doubling before compensating with level.
