## Set the harmonic space

- Sketch a few connected seventh or extended chords on a keys track. Keep common tones and choose a
  top voice that can act as a small tune.
- Remove doubled low notes once the log drum is active. Listen for pad tails or piano releases that
  blur the next chord and shorten the one that overlaps.
- Try a light accent pattern or a slightly staggered chord entrance. Check each chord against the
  drum attack before introducing more timing variation.

## Treat log drum as pitched percussion

- Use a tuned sample or synth source with a short transient and a clearly pitched body. Check its
  root across the notes you plan to play.
- If a synth pitch envelope is available, test a brief downward sweep that resolves to the intended
  pitch early. Read the real parameter schema with `devices_catalog`. If using a sample, audition
  the transient after transposition.
- Program a recognizable syncopated bass phrase with a few holes. Compare the kick alone with kick
  plus log drum. Vary the log drum's lengths or entries where the attack blurs.
- Use shaker accent groups to keep the subdivision moving under the longer bass phrase. Build
  intensity by adding or removing one percussion role at a time.

## Verify the section

- Audition the keys and log drum without the rest of the percussion, then the full groove. Check
  whether the upper chord note and bass contour are both intelligible.
- Listen across the intro to the main groove. A new percussion layer should create motion without
  hiding the main accents.
- Check low-band energy and headroom on the densest bars. Judge pitch, rhythmic spacing and the
  attack by listening before committing the sound.
