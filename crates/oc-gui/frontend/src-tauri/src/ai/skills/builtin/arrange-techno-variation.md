Write a short kick and percussion cycle that holds together dry. Name the
one part whose timing anchors the track. Stretch the arrangement across
several phrases by withholding and reintroducing parts around that anchor.

## Make small changes audible
Introduce one hat accent, filter ride or change in note density at a
phrase boundary. Use one variation per repeat so the core is recognizable.
If two patterns have different lengths, mark a planned joint reset. Listen
on either side of it to check whether it reads as a phrase boundary.

## Compare acid note links
On a resonant saw patch, try `voice.mono: true`, `voice.legato: true` and
`voice.glide: 0.07s`. At 120 BPM, use C3 and G3 eighth notes. Place the second
note 0.75, 0.5, and 0.25 beats after the first in three separate examples.
These give a gap, touching edges, and an overlap. Compare the second attack
and pitch arrival. Keep the overlap only where it gives the intended slide.
Check for the first pitch returning if the second note ends while the first
is still held. For accents, compare one brightened step against its unaccented
copy with the same pitch and linkage. Keep accent and slide decisions separate.

## Make space for returns
For dub techno, keep the chord stab short and send it to a filtered delay.
Lower the return or shorten its low tail if it masks the next kick. For
industrial percussion, try a saturated parallel layer while protecting the
dry attack. Query devices_catalog before writing effect or synth parameters.

## Compare transitions
Place a clear entry, subtraction or filter movement in the last bars of
a phrase and allow space for the returning kick. Write an explicit reset
point on the automation lane when the next phrase needs a different value.
Audition the transition with its tail and on the full mix bus. A move
that reads on a solo track may disappear once the bus processing is active.
