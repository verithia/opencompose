## Choose the reference and roles

- Use a specific style reference to identify meter, phrase length and the low pulse. Match its
  rhythmic roles before borrowing any timing offsets.
- For a bossa nova guitar sketch, put the alternating thumb bass and upper chord attacks on
  independently editable lanes or note lines. Start with a steady bass anchor and syncopated chord
  attacks.
- Give the chord hits short, varied lengths. Repeat one recognizable cell across the chord changes
  and vary its ending when the melody needs a reply.
- For samba percussion, separate low surdo roles from lighter subdivisions. Pick one primary pulse
  and let the contrasting low part answer with a different accent or damping. Leave quieter gaps
  for the top pattern.

## Program the feel

- Use named pads or tracks with distinct tone and decay for open and damped strokes. Compare their
  velocity response before treating louder hits as different articulations.
- Keep a steady count while drawing intentional offsets or accent groups. A blanket swing setting
  moves only its supported offbeats and can disturb a pattern built from other subdivisions.
- Keep subdivisions light and give the bass, hand percussion or guitar a clear lead on each
  accented hit.

## Verify the interaction

- Solo the low roles together to check where the contrast lands. Then add the chord or light
  percussion and hear whether the pulse remains clear.
- Audition a repeat into the next downbeat. Shorten a low tail or chord release if it disguises the
  answering hit.
- Check with the melody before adding more subdivisions. Keep a spare version for a quieter
  section.
