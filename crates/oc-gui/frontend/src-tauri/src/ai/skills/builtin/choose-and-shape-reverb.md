Decide whether the part needs a believable shared space, a short body around
its attack or an audible tail. Hear existing source ambience first; adding
another room cannot remove reflections already recorded into a sample.

## Separate the dry part and the return
Use route-groups-and-returns to retain one intended dry path. A shared return
normally receives a fully wet effect; an insert with a deliberate mix is also
valid. Inspect pre/post-fader tap and existing library reverb before adding it.
Query the actual device for time units, early/tail balance, damping and mix.
Room, plate, hall and spring labels suggest characters, not interchangeable
algorithms. Compare onset density, metallic ringing and decay on the source.
Do not claim a named physical model from a generic reverb preset title.

## Tune the onset and the space
Compare an earlier and later wet onset while the next consonant or hit plays.
Predelay can preserve dry articulation but too much can sound like an echo.
Tempo-derived durations are candidates, not a requirement for a natural room.
Decay describes a falling tail, not an exact stop; hear its overlap with the
next phrase. Keep a useful gap when the arrangement needs one.
Filter the wet path if its low body or high sibilance masks the dry part.
Changing wet EQ does not remove the same content from the dry source.
A darker, quieter or more reverberant part can feel farther away; these cues
interact. More width is not the same as greater front-to-back distance.

## Make a phrase gesture
Automate the feed to select which syllable excites the effect. Closing that
feed leaves an existing tail running. Automate or duck return output when
the accumulated tail must yield to the next line; these are different edits.
Use place-rap-vocal-throws for selected words. Check available detector ports
before promising a vocal-keyed return or control follower behavior.
An impulse response reproduces a captured linear response, not every moving
or nonlinear feature of the original processor. Retain its actual asset.

Compare dry plus wet across the phrase and ending, then in the full mix and
mono. Listen for doubled dry audio, late consonants, buried quiet words and
cut-off tails. Keep the smaller useful effect when extra layers blur the role.
Validate edits and retain source/routing so the accepted space stays editable.
