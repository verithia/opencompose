Start from an authorized phrase and hear it dry. Choose the emotional cue
to keep: a vowel, consonant, breath, glide or word fragment. Separation can
leave room and accompaniment; a chopped separated stem is not a dry recording.
Keep an untouched source and decide whether printed effects belong in the chop.

## Select playable gestures
Mark a few contrasting slices: a clear attack, a stable vowel and a tail.
Automatic transients suggest cuts; they do not identify syllables or intent.
Include enough onset for articulation, and shorten it deliberately for a
softer answer. Keep a longer fragment to prevent every phrase becoming stabs.
Use clip edits for arrangement chops. Re-list after splits before more edits.
Use a sampler only when the desired trigger/mapping behavior is supported;
do not assume slicing automatically creates a playable multi-pad instrument.

## Pitch and shape in context
Confirm the vowel's pitch against the harmony. One monophonic estimate can
misread a glide, overtone or wet tail; audition the sustained region as well.
Compare independent pitch with preserved formants before intentional formant
shifts. Varispeed changes duration too; choose it for that audible tradeoff.
Short fades can prevent clicks, but long fades can remove the consonant.
Use note-off/gate behavior for stops and one-shot behavior for complete gestures.

Write a simple motif, an answer and a gap; vary one endpoint before adding
more slices. Retain the downbeat and phrase return while testing stutters.
At the same retrigger grid, compare repeating one frozen slice with advancing
through neighboring source windows. Vary wording or contour without adding hits.
Print only when a chosen chain should be committed; keep a dry counterpart.
Do not bake a long reverb into every slice unless its rhythmic smear is desired.

## Check the phrase
Hear the chops alone, with chords and with drums at the final tempo.
Listen for wrong roots, chipmunk tone, swallowed attacks, gaps and cut tails.
Compare a dry pass before adding return FX. Duck or automate the return only
if it obscures the next word; use separated-vocal-mixing for wet-source limits.
