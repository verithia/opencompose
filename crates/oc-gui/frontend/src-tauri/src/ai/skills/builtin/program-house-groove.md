Start with a steady kick on all four quarter notes. Add a backbeat and one
offbeat hat voice before programming extra percussion. Loop two bars and
judge the pocket with the bass and chord stabs present.

## Divide the rhythmic space
Give each kick a clear body and shorten any bass notes that run through its
attack. Try the bass entering just after the kick before adding gain ducking.
For an offbeat bass, vary the note ending or leave one offbeat empty rather
than offsetting the entire pattern. A short stab can answer the bass in the
gap. Shorten the stab's envelope or its sample before adding more filtering.

Use a shared nonzero choke group if the closed hat should cut off the open
hat. Check the reverse trigger order too, since either pad in the group can
choke the other. Keep the kick and backbeat fixed while changing the timing
or velocity of a lighter percussion layer. OpenCompose swing shifts offbeat
eighths only, so use explicit positions when individual hits need a nudge.

## Keep space behind the beat
Try a dedicated wet return for selected claps or stabs, with the dry track
still feeding the mix. Start from silence on the return and bring it up
while the whole groove plays. Shorten or filter the tail if it blurs the
next kick. Check the routing in project_extract and the effect controls
in devices_catalog.

Compare the dry and wet versions at a comparable playback level. Listen
for the kick attack, bass pulse, and whether the offbeat still reads.
