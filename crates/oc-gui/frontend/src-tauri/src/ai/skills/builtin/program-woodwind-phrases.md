Choose a job before a patch: principal melody, answer, decorative run or
harmonic support. Start with one verified source and hear its actual attack.
A soft patch can disappear in a dense cue even inside its written pitch range.

## Establish range and gesture
Check sounding pitch and playable range using the loaded instrument's map.
Normalize octave labels when a source calls middle C by another octave number.
Do not infer an available articulation from a note label or a generic preset.
Compare separated notes, a connected phrase and a repeated-note figure at
the intended tempo. Distinguish note overlap from a recorded legato transition.
When switching is unavailable, use separate short and sustained patches.

## Shape a phrase
Choose a destination, breath gap and dynamic contour before adding ornaments.
Verify the actual dynamics, expression and vibrato controls independently.
Use the phrase's musical direction rather than swelling every note equally.
Place a run so it leads into a target and finishes in time for the next role.
Keep rapid repeats when the source can articulate them; do not forbid a whole
instrument family from repeated notes based on an orchestration slogan.

## Share the foreground
Let an answering wind move where the main line rests or holds.
Test a common tone, contrary movement and a different chord tone against the
bass. Parallel thirds are one color, not a universal harmonization rule.
Hand one phrase to another section and compare an overlap with a clean breath.
Avoid duplicating a full section patch on every chord tone unless the apparent
player count and density are intentional. Remove a redundant voice first.

## Verify the cue
Validate and check compatibility, then audition the line, its partner and the
whole section. Include the handoff and the next downbeat in the same window.
Check delayed attacks, abrupt cutoffs, hidden breaths and room tails.
Balance a pre-recorded room layer before adding another shared reverb.
Keep a low woodwind when its line or color contributes; register alone is
not a reason to remove it from a band or hybrid cue.
