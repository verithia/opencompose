Keep the source mix and the first separated take. Align both at the same start.
Find three audition windows: an exposed word plus its tail, a noisy gap,
and a word that overlaps the suspect instrument. Match playback level.
Use the source mix to check what was present before separation.

## Identify the failure before processing
- If the tail vanishes when the added sends and inserts are bypassed, correct
  that routing first. A tail that follows the isolated word in the raw stem
  calls for a different treatment from a wet return you added.
- If identifiable drums or harmony continue through gaps, try manual clip gain
  and short fades between words. Check every boundary for cut breaths and
  consonants. If the bleed overlaps the word, preserve the syllable and
  compare a different separation pass or verified restoration device.
- If sustained vowels chirp or flutter but the source mix doesn't, compare a
  second separation pass before adding compression or brightening the stem.
  Use the same edit points when comparing the two results.
- If a syllable or harmony is missing, check alternate stems and the original
  before deciding whether to patch it. Verify alignment at the patch seam.

## Decide whether the repair serves the new mix
For a baked-in tail, compare a modest repair against the raw stem at the same
level. An external restoration device can be useful when verified in the host.
A gate can reduce the gaps; it cannot recover a dry voice inside a sung note.
Check exposed lines and the densest section. If the repair weakens words more
than it improves clarity, keep the gentler edit and reduce added ambience.
Report the remaining defect and where it is still audible. Use separate
listening passes for the isolated voice and its placement in the mix.
