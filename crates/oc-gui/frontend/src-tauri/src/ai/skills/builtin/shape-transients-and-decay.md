title: Shape transients and decay
when: A drum, pluck or texture needs more attack, less sustain, or control of overly sharp hits.
category: mixing

tools: [read_skill, devices_catalog, project_extract, edit_subtree, effects_add, effects_set_param, effects_set_bypass, render_range, audio_analyze, audio_compare, audition_range]

Name the missing cue. The start may need definition, a peak may be too
sharp, or the tail may be too long for the next note. Listen to isolated
hits, then the phrase, to tell these apart.

For a sampler or synth, compare a single hit with the same source at the phrase's fastest repetition.
Distinguish file length from the energetic body; a long quiet tail may already leave room for the next hit.
Keep the trigger grid fixed while shortening only the source decay or release; retain enough body to hear pitch.
A one-shot ignores note-off: for a kit pad, shorten decay rather than expecting release or MIDI gates to cut it.
Kit-pad decay is seconds to -60 dB; 0 preserves the sample's own decay. It is not an ADSR sustain level or time constant.
If only a few hits are wrong, adjust their source/envelope or gain instead of flattening every hit.

When processing audio, choose a device the catalog actually offers.
A compressor with a slower attack can let the front through. A faster
attack can catch the peak and also remove the cue that locates the hit.
Set release with the rhythm and verify recovery before the next event.
Check whether a crescendo still has quiet intermediate hits after dynamics processing.
Bypass only the suspected insert for diagnosis, preserve the source drive and match the listening output.
Reducing a distorted source's input to match level changes its tone; compensate after that stage instead.

A more prominent front can be achieved by reducing the tail instead of
raising peak level. Avoid pushing the downstream limiter harder just to
hear the change. Check low drums for distortion and bright hits for clicks.

At matched playback level, compare attack shape, space between notes and
level. Use peak and crest as supporting evidence. Keep the edit only if
the result retains its intended character in the arrangement.
