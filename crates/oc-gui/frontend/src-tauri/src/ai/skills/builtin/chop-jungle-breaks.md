Keep the original break available and identify its strongest kicks,
snares and quieter lead-ins by ear. Establish the source downbeat before
lining the clip up with the project. Use the original as a timing reference
while assembling the new phrase on a separate track.

## Slice around the performance
Cut at meaningful transients. Keep at least one short slice that includes
a ghost hit and its following snare so you can move the gesture intact.
Put each intended start at its own clip range and source offset. In
OpenCompose an offset on stretched media is measured in output time.
For a whole-break tempo fit, try percussive stretch and compare the snare
attack against the original. Slice placement can avoid stretching each hit.

Protect the main snare accents across two or four bars. Keep some source
timing, swap one kick or snare pickup, then write a single turnaround.
Tighten only the slices whose tails overlap the next accent. Use a small
edge fade where a click is audible, leaving the attack itself intact.

## Leave room underneath
Write sub notes around the actual kick positions in the rearranged break.
Shorten the low tail of a troublesome slice before thinning the whole break.
Compare the source, edited break alone and edited break plus sub at
matched playback levels. Listen through the loop boundary and the first
downbeat after the turnaround for a missing attack or an unintended double.
