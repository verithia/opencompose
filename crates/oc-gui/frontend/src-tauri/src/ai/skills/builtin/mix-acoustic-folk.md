## Keep the performance continuous

- Identify the lead performance and the supporting take or layer. If the source tracks share bleed,
  listen to a proposed comp splice on both together before replacing only one clip.
- Edit around a phrase boundary when possible. Use a short fade or crossfade and check the first
  consonant, picked transient and the noise floor on both sides of the seam.
- Ride isolated breaths or fret noises with small clip gain changes when distracting. Leave the
  inhalation or finger movement that signals a phrase.
- For a sampled backing part, reduce its density and release overlap under words before changing
  the lead sound.

## Balance with small edits

- Listen to a low guitar note, a finger attack and a sung phrase together. If one resonance keeps
  jumping out, confirm it on several notes and try a narrow cut before broad tonal changes.
- Use a gentle low cut only as far as rumble or boom improves. Compare at similar level and check
  the guitar body as well as the voice.
- If compression buries a quiet finger pattern or pulls breaths forward, try phrase gain first and
  retest with less gain reduction.
- Let captured ambience establish the space. Add a short shared return only where dry overdubs feel
  detached, and check the line endings.

## Verify the edit

- Audition a quiet exposed phrase, a dense phrase and the seam between them. Compare mono if
  multi-mic or doubled sources lose focus together.
- Listen to the first transient after each edit and to the end of the fade. Validate the clip edits
  and keep the original performance available for comparison.
