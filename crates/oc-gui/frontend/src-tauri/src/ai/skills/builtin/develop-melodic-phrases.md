title: Develop a motif into a melodic phrase
when: A melody loops without direction, sounds random, or needs a contrasting answer and a convincing section ending.
category: production
version: 1
tools: [read_skill, project_extract, edit_subtree, project_validate, audition_range]

Identify a short recognizable cell in the existing part: its rhythm, contour,
accent or ending. Preserve one of those identities while changing another.
Hear it with the real bass and chords before replacing the sound.

## Make an answer
Keep the opening rhythm and vary the last notes of the next phrase. Compare
a repeated tone, a stepwise answer and one deliberate leap in the same register.
Choose by the direction each creates against the harmony, rather than a rule
that more motion or more notes is better. Let an important leap have room.
A rest can make the next entrance speak more clearly than a longer fill.
Keep the supporting figure fixed when comparing a held lead with rearticulated answers.
Design gates per event: a longer chord anchor need not lengthen passing notes.
Check strong or sustained notes against the actual chords. A passing tension
can work through its preparation or exit; being outside the scale alone does
not make a note wrong, and being inside it does not guarantee a useful landing.

## Shape the phrase ending
Compare an open ending with a more settled one and hear the following entrance.
The melody's last pitch, bass and chord together determine the effect.
Try holding the motif while slowing the chord changes, then retaining the
chords and changing the motif ending. This distinguishes harmonic rhythm
from melodic activity. Keep those comparisons at the same sound and level.
Use a section boundary to change register, note density or accompaniment;
changing every layer at once can erase the identity you meant to develop.

## Turn a loop into a section
Give an earlier repeat fewer answers or a shorter tail; save a variation for
a later repeat. Write a countermelody in the lead's gaps before doubling it.
Check whether an octave doubling reinforces the same line or competes with it.
In an ensemble return, test melody only when other parts now supply its harmony.
For a delayed answer, choose register, omissions and ending as a new part.
Hear its release with the next entrance, keeping the original line recognizable.
Shape an arp's register apex, velocity contour and breathing gaps separately.
Audition the paired phrases with their pickup and next downbeat. Then hear the
section without interruption for repetition, breathing room and direction.
Inspect written starts, note lengths and dynamic contour when an intended
accent is missing. Adjust the part before adding modulation to conceal it.
Validate the edit. Keep the variation whose role is clear in the arrangement,
and read writing-notes for positioning, dynamics and phrase boundaries.
