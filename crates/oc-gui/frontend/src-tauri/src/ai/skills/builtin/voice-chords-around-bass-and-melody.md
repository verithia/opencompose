Separate the tonal centre, sounding bass, chord root and top melody. They can
be different notes. Read establish-a-modal-center for tonic and modal tests.
Read written pitches when available; a chord label or
strong spectral peak does not establish the full voicing.

## Test the uncertain relationship
Keep a simple keyboard sound, rhythm and level fixed. For an inversion,
compare C4 E4 G4 over C3, then over E3: the upper chord stays major while
the bass changes. To test quality, restore C3 and compare E4 with Eb4.
Do not change inversion, quality and register together and call the result
proof of one cause. A slash bass need not be a chord member.
For external music hear bass and top separately. analyze_notes is monophonic;
use it as a candidate for one voice, never as a complete chord transcription.
Mark unresolved inner tones instead of naming an elaborate chord confidently.

## Give each voice a job
Keep the chosen bass and melody, then place defining thirds and sevenths.
Try omitting a duplicated root or nonessential fifth before adding register.
Give sustained shells, pulsed middle voices and top notes distinct jobs.
Not every layer needs the full chord or the same rhythm. Test a fixed arp or
pedal against each changing bass, rather than relying on a shared scale label.
Duplicate same-pitch events and brief release overlaps are not proof of useful harmony.
A bass already carrying the root can leave the upper part more room.
Compare a close voicing with one that spreads the same pitches across octaves.
Dense low notes can blur on one patch yet work on a shorter or thinner sound.
Retain useful common tones, and move other inner voices to nearby destinations.
For staggered attacks, compare equal gates with different gates ending together.
Preserve the intended arrival and release, then hear the next bass change.
Check every resulting interval against the actual bass and held melody;
minimal movement is a choice, not a reason to keep an unwanted clash.

## Extend and connect
Add one extension to a known chord and hear it in the progression. Spell
its pitch before assigning a symbol; an omitted third can leave quality open.
For a temporary dominant, choose the destination first and check the altered
third/seventh and their motion. A local target does not establish a new key.
Audition the whole phrase and loop seam with bass and lead. Listen for bass
direction, top-line continuity and whether inner density masks the melody.
Validate the written notes and keep the simpler voicing when added colour
works only in isolation. Read writing-notes for the actual phrase syntax.
