Choose a specific reference feel before programming. State whether the tempo
is counted at the fast drum pulse or its half-time interpretation.
Read assemble-a-sampled-drum-kit for actual pads, layers and choke behavior.

## Establish the anchors
For a basic two-step control in 4/4, put the snare on beats 2 and 4.
Try kick hits on beat 1 and the second eighth of beat 3, then hear the loop.
Use writing-notes for exact pad and beat/subdivision syntax.
This is one starting pattern, not a definition of every drum and bass style.
Compare one displaced kick or missing accent while keeping the snare reference.
A half-time variant changes the heard backbeat; do not infer it from BPM alone.

## Build the moving layer
Add a short hat or shuffle gesture below the main snare's perceived level.
Differentiate ghost pickups from backbeats by actual articulation and velocity.
Keep a useful two-hit gesture intact before splitting every transient.
Read chop-jungle-breaks when the groove comes from recorded break slices.
Check the tail before and after a slice; two hats can carry different snare
spill and are not interchangeable merely because their labels match.
Choose eighth or sixteenth swing deliberately and avoid swinging an already
swung source again. Move only the attacks that need relative timing changes.

## Develop two or four bars
Repeat the anchor phrase, vary one pickup and reserve a turnaround for the end.
Try a short tom, kick displacement or snare roll rather than filling all voices.
Keep enough silence for the next main snare and the returning downbeat.
Use a unique variant when only one repeat should change.
Check rapid hits for lost sample layers, unintended choke or overlapping lows.

## Verify against the bass
Validate and audition the kit alone, with the actual bass and in the drop.
Include the fill and first beat after it. Compare at the same playback level.
Shorten a competing low tail or change one kick before filtering the whole kit.
Keep the main snare recognizable under the bass; read unmask-kick-and-bass
for a justified timing, envelope or band-specific ducking repair.
Check a quieter section as well as the climax so variation has a direction.
