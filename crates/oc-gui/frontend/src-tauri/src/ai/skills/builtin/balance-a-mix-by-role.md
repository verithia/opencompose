Choose a representative passage and name its focal part, rhythmic anchor,
low foundation and supporting answers. Inspect existing gain and routing.
Hear the unaltered mix before soloing; keep a recoverable comparison.

## Establish a useful hierarchy
Set the focal part against the anchor, then bring supports in by function.
A quiet bed may carry continuity rather than demand separate attention.
Equal peaks, equal loudness or a full spectrum do not make a useful balance.
If a part vanishes, hear it with its main competitor. Try level, register,
shorter notes or another sample before adding a chain to every track.
A bass can read through harmonics while a kick supplies low weight, or vice
versa. Use unmask-kick-and-bass for that choice, not fixed crossover numbers.
A spectrum dip can be intentional. Silence and a narrow texture can make the
next entrance stronger; do not fill every gap with noise or another pad.

## Separate the remaining problems
A phrase-level jump may need an automation ride; a sharp peak or recovery
problem may need dynamics. Read hear-compression-and-recovery for that test.
An isolated click, plosive or bad seam needs a local edit, not global dulling.
A source that sounds right alone but masks the lead needs an overlap decision;
a source with the wrong articulation or pitch needs source or note work first.
Use space to place a part behind another once the dry balance is useful.
Read choose-and-shape-reverb for onset, density, tail and return choices.

## Follow the song's journey
Compare sparse and dense passages, entrances and final chorus at one monitor
gain. Let the focal role change; bring an introduction forward, then let it
recede for a response. Do not make every section equally full by default.
Use write-mix-automation for deliberate section rides. Confirm source trims
and track faders are at the intended positions relative to nonlinear inserts.
Lowering every track can change bus drive; a final monitor trim cannot repair
clipping already printed upstream. Preserve raw levels when diagnosing it.

Compare the complete phrase, next entrance and stereo/mono balance. Use
metrics to locate peaks or loss, not to score musical hierarchy. Restore all
temporary solos. Validate the edit and retain only changes with a clear role.
Read master-for-delivery after the mix holds together through the whole song.
